ATX vs mATX vs ITX: which size do you need?

Cabinet size follows motherboard size. A bigger cabinet accepts smaller boards, but never the other way round. Exact GPU length and radiator limits vary by model, so always check the product page before you order.

Airflow vs glass: what matters for gaming

A gaming PC turns hundreds of watts into heat, mostly from the graphics card. A cabinet with a mesh or well-vented front lets the intake fans pull in cool air directly. A sealed glass front looks clean but forces air in through narrow side or bottom gaps, so the fans work harder. Tempered glass on the side panel is fine; it is the front panel that decides airflow.

For fans, three is a practical minimum for a gaming build: two intakes at the front and one exhaust at the rear. High-end GPUs and 120W-plus CPUs benefit from more. Many cabinets now ship with four to seven ARGB fans, which saves you ₹1,000 or more over buying fans separately.

How many fans does a gaming cabinet need?

Three fans (two front intake, one rear exhaust) is enough for most mid-range builds. For a high-end GPU, add a third intake or top exhaust. More fans than that mainly add looks and noise, unless you are running a large radiator.

A note on cabinets that come with a power supply

Some budget cabinets, like the Circle Bonsai Plus 3.0 and Bonsai Vista 3.0, ship with a bundled power supply. That is fine for an office or home PC. For a gaming build with a dedicated graphics card, use a separate, quality PSU instead, because the power supply feeds every component and a weak one is the part most likely to cause crashes or damage. A 650W 80 Plus Bronze unit like the Ant Esports RX650 (₹3,799) or DeepCool PL650D (₹4,990) suits most mid-range builds, while the Antec G750W 80 Plus Gold (₹7,999) gives headroom for bigger cards. Check GPU clearance before you buy

Modern triple-fan cards are long. A card like the MSI RTX 5070 Ti Gaming Trio OC Plus needs a cabinet with generous GPU clearance, which usually means an ATX mid tower or larger. Compare the cabinet's listed maximum GPU length with your card's length, and leave room for the front fans and any radiator. Frequently asked questions

Which is the best cabinet for a gaming PC under ₹5,000?

The Ant Value CV200 (₹2,999, three 120mm fans) and Ant Sports Elite 1120 (₹2,999, four ARGB fans) are the best value picks for Micro-ATX and budget builds. If you want a full mid tower, the Ant Esports SX7 White costs ₹4,466.

Should I buy an ATX or mATX cabinet for gaming?

Choose an ATX mid tower if you have an ATX motherboard, a long triple-fan graphics card or plan to add a large radiator. A Micro-ATX cabinet works well for budget and mid-range builds with a Micro-ATX board and a mid-length GPU, and takes less desk space.

Is a glass front cabinet bad for gaming?

Not necessarily, but a mesh or vented front moves air more easily. A glass-front case like the Antec C5 relies on side or bottom intakes and more fans, which is why it ships with seven 120mm fans. Side glass panels do not affect airflow much.

Can I use the power supply that comes with a cabinet for gaming?

It is not recommended. Bundled power supplies in budget cabinets suit office PCs. For a gaming build with a dedicated graphics card, buy a separate 80 Plus certified PSU, such as a 650W Bronze unit for mid-range builds or a 750W Gold unit for higher-end cards.
