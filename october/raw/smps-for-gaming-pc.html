What wattage SMPS do I need for a gaming PC?

Add GPU board power, CPU maximum power and about 60W for the rest, then add 40-50% headroom. Most 1080p builds with an RTX 5060, RX 7600 or RTX 5060 Ti land at 550W to 650W. An RTX 5070 Ti or RX 9070 XT needs 750W, an RTX 5080 850W, and RTX 5090 class systems 1000W or more.

How to size an SMPS: the method we use

Add three numbers: the GPU's rated board power (published by Nvidia and AMD), the CPU's maximum package power (AMD's PPT or Intel's maximum turbo power), and about 60-75W for the motherboard, RAM, SSDs and fans. That is your estimated peak draw. Multiply it by 1.4 to 1.5 and round up to the next common SMPS size.

Headroom matters because modern GPUs spike well above rated power for milliseconds, and an SMPS near its limit trips first. A unit at 50-60% load also sits in its efficiency sweet spot and runs cooler in a Delhi summer.

80 Plus ratings explained

The 80 Plus badge tells you how much power drawn from the wall reaches your components. The rest becomes heat inside the SMPS. The 50% load figure is the one to compare:

On India's 230V mains the same unit usually runs a point or two more efficiently. Bronze vs Gold is roughly 20-30W at the wall under a 400W gaming load, so Gold earns its price mainly on 750W+ builds, quieter fans and longer warranties rather than your electricity bill.

Avoid unbranded SMPS for a gaming build

The unbranded "450W" unit bundled with many cabinets is fine for an office PC, but the wrong place to save money on a gaming build. Such units often print peak rather than continuous wattage, skip over-current, over-voltage and short-circuit protection, and carry no 80 Plus certification. For any build with a graphics card, buy a branded unit rated at least 80 Plus Bronze. 80 Plus White units like the Circle 500W are a sensible choice for office and iGPU systems.

Modular, semi-modular or non-modular?

Non-modular units have every cable fixed: cheaper, but unused cables must be tucked away. Semi-modular units fix only the 24-pin and CPU cables. Fully modular units let you remove every cable, which helps cable management and upgrades. Semi-modular is the practical middle ground for a glass-panel cabinet; go fully modular for compact or showpiece builds.

Do I need an ATX 3.1 SMPS for an RTX 50 series card?

Not strictly. Cards with the 16-pin 12V-2x6 (formerly 12VHPWR) connector ship with an 8-pin adapter. A native ATX 3.0 or 3.1 unit is still better for RTX 5070 Ti and above: one cable instead of three or four, and a design built to handle short power spikes of up to twice its rated output.

ATX 3.0, ATX 3.1 and the 12V-2x6 connector

ATX 3.0 introduced the 16-pin 12VHPWR connector, which delivers up to 600W through one cable, and set rules for handling sudden GPU power spikes. RTX 40 cards popularised it. ATX 3.1 moved to the revised 12V-2x6 connector, whose shorter sense pins stop the card drawing full power unless the plug is fully seated. The two are cross-compatible.

Push the plug in until it sits flush, and do not bend the cable sharply near the connector. Entry RTX 50 cards and most Radeon cards still use regular 8-pin connectors, so check your card's listing before paying extra for ATX 3.1.

Frequently asked questions

Which SMPS is best for a gaming PC in India?

For most 1080p and 1440p builds, a branded 650W 80 Plus Bronze or Gold unit such as the DeepCool PL650D (₹4,990) or Antec G650 (₹6,499) is the best balance. Step up to 750W for an RTX 5070 Ti or RX 9070 XT, and 850W for an RTX 5080.

Is 650W enough for a gaming PC?

Yes, for a Ryzen 5 or Core i5 paired with a graphics card rated up to about 200W, such as the RTX 5060, RTX 5060 Ti, RX 7600 or RX 9060 XT. Cards like the RTX 5070 Ti, RX 9070 XT and RTX 5080 need 750W to 850W.

Is 80 Plus Bronze good enough for gaming, or do I need Gold?

80 Plus Bronze is fine for budget and mainstream gaming builds up to 650W. Gold is worth it for 750W and above, for long daily sessions, or if you want a quieter fan and a longer warranty. Skip any unit with no 80 Plus certification for a gaming PC.

Do I need a new SMPS for an RTX 50 series card?

Not always. If your current unit is branded, certified and has enough wattage for the card, you can use the adapter supplied with the GPU. For RTX 5070 Ti and above, an ATX 3.0 or 3.1 unit with a native 12V-2x6 or 12VHPWR cable is cleaner and handles power spikes better.

Can I use the SMPS that came with my cabinet for gaming?

We do not recommend it for any build with a dedicated graphics card. Bundled and unbranded units often overstate their wattage, lack proper protection circuits and carry no 80 Plus certification. Keep them for office or iGPU systems and buy a certified branded SMPS for gaming.
