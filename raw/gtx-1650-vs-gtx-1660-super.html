The Nvidia GTX 1650 has been the default budget graphics card in Indian gaming builds for years, and buyers still ask whether the Nvidia GTX 1650 or the step-up GTX 1660 Super is the smarter pick in 2026. Both are older Turing-generation cards that skip modern features like DLSS 3 and ray tracing, but they remain fully capable of 1080p esports. The Nvidia GTX 1650 is a 4GB card aimed at low-settings 1080p gaming, while the GTX 1660 Super adds 6GB of faster GDDR6 and noticeably more shader power. If you already own a Nvidia GTX 1650, the honest answer is that it still runs CS2, Valorant, GTA V, and older single-player titles at playable frame rates, so the real question is how much headroom you actually want.

Is the GTX 1650 or GTX 1660 Super better in 2026?

The GTX 1660 Super is clearly the stronger of the two: its 6GB of GDDR6 and higher shader count deliver roughly 40 to 50 percent more performance than the 4GB GTX 1650 at 1080p, which matters in newer titles. That said, both are legacy cards. If you are buying new in 2026, the in-stock Nvidia RTX 3050 at ₹27,999 is the smarter budget choice because it adds DLSS and modern encoders the older GPUs lack.

GTX 1650 vs GTX 1660 Super: what actually separates them

Both cards launched in the same Turing family, but they sit a full tier apart. The GTX 1650 uses 4GB of memory and a cut-down GPU meant for entry 1080p play, so it holds up in esports at low to medium settings but runs into its 4GB VRAM limit in newer AAA games. The GTX 1660 Super steps up to 6GB of faster GDDR6 and a much wider shader array, which is why it lands close to a 60fps target at 1080p in far more titles. For older or competitive games, either works. For newer releases, the extra VRAM and horsepower of the 1660 Super is the difference between playable and stuttery.

Should I still buy a GTX 1650 in 2026?

Only if you find one cheap for a light esports or office build, or as a like-for-like replacement in an old system. For a fresh gaming PC, no: the 4GB VRAM and lack of DLSS and ray tracing hold it back in newer games. The in-stock RTX 3050 at ₹27,999 or the RX 7600 at ₹33,999 give you a modern feature set that will age far better.

Frequently asked questions

Which graphic card is best for gaming on a budget in 2026?

For a new budget build, the in-stock Nvidia RTX 3050 at ₹27,999 is the best-value pick, since it adds DLSS and ray tracing that the older GTX 1650 and GTX 1660 Super lack. If you can stretch a little, the AMD RX 7600 at ₹33,999 with 8GB of VRAM is the next step up for 1080p high settings.

What is the best Nvidia graphic card for gaming under ₹30,000?

The Nvidia RTX 3050 6GB at ₹27,999 is the best Nvidia graphic card for gaming in that budget from our current in-stock range. It comfortably handles 1080p gaming with DLSS support, and it replaces the older GTX 1650 and 1660 Super as the sensible entry Nvidia option.

Is the GTX 1660 Super much faster than the GTX 1650?

Yes. The GTX 1660 Super is roughly 40 to 50 percent faster than the GTX 1650 at 1080p thanks to its wider shader array and 6GB of faster GDDR6 memory versus the 1650's 4GB. That gap is the difference between a stable 60fps and frequent dips in newer titles.

Are the GTX 1650 and GTX 1660 Super in stock at Avika Retails?

Both are currently out of stock as newer GPUs have replaced them, so their product pages are best used to check whether stock has returned. For an available budget card today, the Nvidia RTX 3050 and the AMD RX 7600 are the closest in-stock options.

Can the GTX 1650 run modern AAA games?

It can run many of them, but only at low settings and often below 60fps, and its 4GB of VRAM limits texture quality in newer releases. For older and esports titles it remains fine, but for current AAA games a 6GB or 8GB card like the RTX 3050 or RX 7600 is a far better fit.
